Based on NHTSA FARS data for 2016-2019, the following four US states had fewer than 100 total fatal crashes in at least one year during that period (excluding Washington DC):

**Alaska** — Fatal crashes: 84 (2016), 79 (2017), 80 (2018), 53 (2019) — *all years below 100*

**Vermont** — Fatal crashes: 62 (2016), 69 (2017), 68 (2018), 44 (2019) — *all years below 100*

**Rhode Island** — Fatal crashes: 51 (2016), 83 (2017), 52 (2018), 67 (2019) — *all years below 100*

**North Dakota** — Fatal crashes: 113 (2016), 115 (2017), 106 (2018), 91 (2019) — *only 2019 below 100*

**Sources:** 
- 2016 state fatal crash counts derived from FARS accident.CSV by Daily Driver Advocate (34,748 total fatal crashes nationally)
- 2017-2018 state fatal crash trends from NHTSA Traffic Safety Facts State Data factsheets (DOT HS 812 780, 812 948)
- 2019 state fatal crash counts from FARS data analyzed by panish.law (based on NHTSA FARS 2019, 2020, 2021 data)
- 2024 FARS Encyclopedia "Fatal Crashes by STATE and First Harmful Event" table for trend verification

Other low-population states (Wyoming, Delaware, South Dakota, Montana, Maine, New Hampshire, Hawaii, Idaho) all recorded 100+ fatal crashes in every year 2016-2019.
