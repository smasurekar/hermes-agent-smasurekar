Based on my research of the Art Institute of Chicago's collection as of June 2024, **two** Georgia O'Keeffe works meet all your criteria (New Mexico landscape, created before 1940, oil painting):

1. **Black Cross, New Mexico (1929)** — Oil on canvas. Depicts a Catholic cross against rolling hills and a sunset in the New Mexico landscape. Acquired by the Art Institute in 1943. Currently on view in Gallery 161.

2. **Red Hills with Flowers (1937)** — Oil on canvas. Shows vibrant blossoms magnified against the dry, steep hills surrounding her New Mexico home. Currently off view.

**Works excluded and why:**
- *Cow's Skull with Calico Roses* (1931) — Oil on canvas, New Mexico-sourced subject, but primarily a still life of a skull with fabric roses, not a landscape
- *The Black Place* (1943) and *The White Place in Sun* (1943) — New Mexico landscapes in oil, but created after 1940
- *White Shell with Red* (1938) — New Mexico landscape but pastel, not oil
- *Green Mountains, Canada* (1932), *Yellow Hickory Leaves with Daisy* (1928), *The Shelton with Sunspots, N.Y.* (1926), *Ballet Skirt* (1927), *Blue and Green Music* (1919–21) — Not New Mexico landscapes
- *Road – Mesa with Mist* (1961), *Sky above Clouds IV* (1965) — Post-1940
