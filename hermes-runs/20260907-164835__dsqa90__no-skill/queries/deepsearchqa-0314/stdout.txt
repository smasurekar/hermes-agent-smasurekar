As of **May 2024**, the genus ***Tyrannosaurus*** has the following formally named species, with their taxonomic status:

---

### **1. Currently Valid / Generally Accepted**

| Species | Authority | Year | Status |
|---------|-----------|------|--------|
| ***Tyrannosaurus rex*** | Osborn | 1905 | **Type species** — universally accepted, only uncontested species. |

---

### **2. Formally Published in 2024 (Pre-May 2024)**

| Species | Authority | Year | Status |
|---------|-----------|------|--------|
| ***Tyrannosaurus mcraeensis*** | Dalman et al. | 2024 | **Published Jan 2024** (*Scientific Reports*). Holotype NMMNH P-3698 (Hall Lake Formation, New Mexico). **Validity contested**: multiple 2025 studies (Voris et al., Morrison et al., Carr) argue it falls within *T. rex* variation and treat it as a junior synonym. As of May 2024 it was a newly erected, published name, but its acceptance was already debated. |

---

### **3. Controversial / Not Widely Accepted (2022 Proposal)**

| Species | Authority | Year | Status |
|---------|-----------|------|--------|
| ***Tyrannosaurus imperator*** | Paul et al. | 2022 | Proposed in *Evolutionary Biology*; holotype = “Sue” (FMNH PR2081). **Rejected by most tyrannosaur specialists** (Brusatte, Carr, Holtz, Hone, O’Connor, Zanno, et al.) as inadequately supported; variation attributed to ontogeny/individual differences. |
| ***Tyrannosaurus regina*** | Paul et al. | 2022 | Same paper; holotype = “Wankel rex” (MOR 555). **Same rejection** as *T. imperator*. |

> **Note:** Paul maintained both names in a 2025 follow-up, but the broader community has not adopted them.

---

---

### **Summary (as of May 2024)**

| Category | Count |
|----------|-------|
| **Universally accepted valid species** | **1** (*T. rex*) |
| **Newly published, validity actively debated** | **1** (*T. mcraeensis*) |
| **Proposed but rejected by consensus** | **2** (*T. imperator*, *T. regina*) |
| **Historically named, now invalid** | **10+** |

**Bottom line:** Only ***Tyrannosaurus rex*** is uncontroversially valid. ***T. mcraeensis*** exists as a published 2024 name but was already facing strong synonymy arguments by early 2025. The 2022 “three-species” split (*imperator*, *regina*) is not accepted by the tyrannosaur research community.
