Based on the 2023 Autism Speaks "Autism by the Numbers" report and 2023 U.S. Census Bureau poverty data (via Wikipedia's sourced table), here are the **10 states with the oldest average age of first autism intervention**, ranked by their **percentage of population below the poverty line (highest to lowest)**:

| Rank | State | Avg. Age of First Intervention (2016–2019 NSCH) | 2023 Poverty Rate (%) |
|------|-------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Mississippi | 5.5 years | 18.0 |
| 2 | West Virginia | 7.2 years | 16.7 |
| 3 | Oklahoma | 5.3 years | 15.9 |
| 4 | Alabama | 4.9 years | 15.6 |
| 5 | Ohio | 5.6 years | 13.3 |
| 6 | Montana | 5.9 years | 11.7 |
| 7 | Iowa | 5.0 years | 11.3 |
| 8 | Connecticut | 5.0 years | 10.3 |
| 9 | North Dakota | 6.0 years | 9.8 |
| 10 | Colorado | 5.0 years | 9.3 |

**Sources**
- Autism Speaks 2023 Annual Report (NSCH 2016–2019) — average age of first intervention by state: [autismspeaks.org/sites/default/files/ABN_Annual_Report_2023.pdf](https://www.autismspeaks.org/sites/default/files/ABN_Annual_Report_2023.pdf)
- U.S. Census Bureau 2023 poverty rates (American Community Survey 1-year estimates) as compiled by Wikipedia: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_poverty_rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_poverty_rate#2023_U.S._Census_Bureau_table)

**Note**: The Autism Speaks report uses parent-reported data from the 2016–2019 National Survey of Children's Health; the poverty rates are official 2023 Census Bureau estimates. Several states (Alaska, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Nebraska) also reported 4.9 years; Alabama is shown here as the next-highest-poverty state among those tied at 4.9 years.
