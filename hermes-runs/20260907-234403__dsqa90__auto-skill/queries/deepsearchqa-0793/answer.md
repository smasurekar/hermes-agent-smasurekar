Based on my research, here's the answer:

**Chile** was the latest to join the Allies side in WWII.

**Reasoning:**

1. **League of Nations founding members (January 10, 1920)** — Chile was explicitly listed as an original member on that date.

2. **Original 51 UN members (1945)** — Chile was among the 51 founding members, signing the UN Charter on June 26, 1945.

3. **Date joining the Allies** — Among the intersection of these two groups, Chile declared war on Japan on **April 11–13, 1945** (Congress approved April 11, president signed April 13). This was later than Argentina's March 27, 1945 declaration, and far later than all other common members (most joined 1939–1942).

No other country in the intersection declared war later—other South American nations like Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay, and Venezuela declared war in February 1945; European neutrals like Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt (which joined the League later, not on Jan 10, 1920) declared war in February–March 1945.
