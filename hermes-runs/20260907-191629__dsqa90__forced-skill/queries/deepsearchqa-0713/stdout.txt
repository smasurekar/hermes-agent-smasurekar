Based on my research using authoritative NCES sources, here are the **5 states with less than 1 million public K-12 students in 2021 that had the lowest percentage of private school students**:

## Answer

| Rank | State | Public Enrollment | Private Enrollment | Total Enrollment | Private % |
|------|-------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Wyoming** | 93,093 | 1,681 | 94,774 | **1.77%** |
| 2 | **Utah** | 690,934 | 15,769 | 706,703 | **2.23%** |
| 3 | **Alaska** | 129,944 | 4,468 | 134,412 | **3.32%** |
| 4 | **West Virginia** | 252,720 | 11,741 | 264,461 | **4.44%** |
| 5 | **Arkansas** | 489,565 | 23,334 | 512,899 | **4.55%** |

## Methodology
- **Public enrollment**: NCES Common Core of Data (CCD), State Nonfiscal Survey 2021–22 (Provisional v1a) — actual Fall 2021 membership counts
- **Private enrollment**: NCES Private School Universe Survey (PSS) 2021–22 — K-12 enrollment (excludes prekindergarten)
- **Filter**: States with public enrollment < 1,000,000 (35 states qualified; DC excluded)
- **Calculation**: Private % = Private ÷ (Public + Private) × 100

## Sources
1. Public enrollment by state (Fall 2021): https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/tables/202122_summary_2.asp
2. Private enrollment by state (2021–22, K-12): https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/pss/tables/TABLE15fl2122.asp

**Note**: Private school percentages are notably low in these states. Wyoming has the lowest at 1.77% — only 1,681 private school students out of ~95,000 total. Utah's is similarly low at 2.23% despite having the largest public enrollment among the five (690,934).
