Based on my research of NHTSA FARS data (via the official FARS Encyclopedia and the farsapi.com portal that publishes NHTSA FARS data), here are the US states (excluding DC) that had **fewer than 100 total fatal crashes in at least one year between 2016–2019**:

## States with <100 fatal crashes in at least one year (2016–2019)

| State | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | Years <100 |
|-------|------|------|------|------|------------|
| **Alaska** | ~80–84* | 75 | 69 | 62 | All 4 years |
| **Vermont** | ~60–62* | 63 | 60 | 44 | All 4 years |
| **Rhode Island** | ~50–51* | 77 | 56 | 53 | All 4 years |
| **North Dakota** | ~108–110* | 106 | 95 | 91 | 2018, 2019 |
| **South Dakota** | ~110–114* | 111 | 110 | 88 | 2019 only |
| **New Hampshire** | ~130–133* | 98 | 134 | 90 | 2017, 2019 |
| **Hawaii** | ~115–118* | 96 | 110 | 102 | 2017 only |

*2016 values are estimated from NHTSA-reported fatalities (fatal crashes are typically 2–5% lower than fatalities for small states). The 2017–2019 fatal crash counts come directly from NHTSA FARS via farsapi.com.

## States that **never** fell below 100 fatal crashes in 2016–2019
- Wyoming (lowest: 100 in 2018)
- Delaware (lowest: 104 in 2018)
- Maine (lowest: 127 in 2018)
- Idaho (lowest: 201 in 2019)
- Montana (lowest: 166 in 2019)
- Nebraska (lowest: 201 in 2018)
