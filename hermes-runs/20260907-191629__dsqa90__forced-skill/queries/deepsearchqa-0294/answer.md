Based on my extensive research across Statistics Canada tables, utility annual reports, and government publications, **Quebec** had the largest total capital expenditure in hydroelectric power generation in 2022/2023.

## Key Evidence

**Quebec (Hydro-Québec):**
- $4.9 billion invested in 2023 alone (up from ~$4.2 billion in 2022) [1]
- Owns 61 hydroelectric dams with 38,400 MW capacity — over half of Canada's total [2]
- 2035 Action Plan outlines $90-110 billion in additional hydro investments [1]

**British Columbia (BC Hydro):**
- ~$3.9 billion in 2022/23 (fiscal year ending March 31, 2023) [3]
- Site C project ($16B total) contributed ~$1.6B in 2022/23 [3]

**Manitoba (Manitoba Hydro):**
- Capital expenditures of ~$500-600M/year for sustaining hydro assets [4]
- Keeyask project largely completed by 2022

**Newfoundland & Labrador:**
- Infrastructure spending decreased 30.6% in 2022 due to project completions [5]
- Muskrat Falls ($13B+) was mostly complete by 2022

## Why Quebec Leads
1. **Scale**: Hydro-Québec's hydro capacity (38,400 MW) dwarfs other provincial hydro fleets
2. **Ongoing expansion**: Romaine complex (1,550 MW) completed in 2023 after $6.5B+ investment
3. **Sustainment + growth**: 2023 spending split between asset sustainment and new generation/transmission
4. **National share**: Quebec produces ~51% of Canada's hydroelectricity [6]

## Data Limitations
Statistics Canada tables 34-10-0035-01 and 34-10-0063-02 contain the detailed NAICS 221111 (hydro-electric power generation) capital expenditure by province, but require custom table extraction. The published Daily releases aggregate at the "electric power generation, transmission and distribution" (NAICS 2211) level, not hydro-specific.
