Based on my research, the "earlier case" is **Gamble v. United States** (2019), where Justice Gorsuch and Justice Ginsburg both dissented from the majority's upholding of the dual sovereignty doctrine. 

In her dissent in *Gamble*, Justice Ginsburg explicitly identifies the "twice-affirmed" precedent as the two 1959 cases that reaffirmed the dual sovereignty doctrine originally established in *United States v. Lanza* (1922). She writes:

> "In *United States* v. *Lanza*, 260 U.S. 377 (1922), this Court held that 'an act denounced as a crime by both national and state sovereignties is an offense against the peace and dignity of both and may be punished by each.' Id., at 382. Decades later, a sharply divided Court **reaffirmed this separate-sovereigns doctrine. *Abbate* v. *United States*, 359 U.S. 187 (1959); *Bartkus* v. *Illinois*, 359 U.S. 121 (1959). I would not cling to those ill-advised decisions."

The majority opinion in *Gamble* likewise notes: "When petitioners in 1959 asked us **twice** to reverse course, **we twice refused**... *Abbate* v. *United States*, 359 U.S. 187, 195; see also *Bartkus* v. *Illinois*, 359 U.S. 121."

**Answer:** The twice-affirmed precedent Justice Ginsburg cited and felt should be overturned consists of **two companion cases decided the same day in 1959**: ***Abbate v. United States*, 359 U.S. 187 (1959)** and ***Bartkus v. Illinois*, 359 U.S. 121 (1959)**. She refers to them collectively as "those ill-advised decisions" that reaffirmed the dual sovereignty doctrine.
